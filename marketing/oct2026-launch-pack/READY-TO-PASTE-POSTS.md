## Keja AI case study — LinkedIn + X

### LinkedIn — long-form (2,139 characters, limit 3,000)

> Every property deal comes down to three questions:
> — Is the title genuine?
> — Is the price honest?
> — Is the investment sound?
>
> In Kenya, answering them has meant stitching together agents, banks, lawyers and spreadsheets — each holding one slice of the picture, none able to see the whole.
>
> So when CHACADOM INVESTMENTS built Keja AI — a platform covering the full property lifecycle, Discover → Verify → Analyse → Finance → Invest → Transact → Manage — my mandate was the AI layer that answers those three questions with evidence.
>
> What I built:
> → "Ask Keja AI" — a conversational assistant grounded in the platform's actual listings and market data, answering with the user's role in mind: buyer, seller, landlord, tenant, investor, developer, bank, institution
> → Investment scoring on every listing — an explainable number computed from each property's structured attributes and market context
> → Title & document verification tooling — structured checks with traceable evidence, feeding the KEJA Trust Score
>
> Why the architecture matters: answers, scores and transaction records all draw from the same structured data spine — so they cannot quietly disagree with each other. In high-stakes products, that coherence is what makes a trust score mean something.
>
> The platform is live in public trial at keja.app:
> • 87+ live listings
> • 12 Kenyan counties
> • 70 listings certified at Trust Score 80+
> • 8 stakeholder workspaces on one platform
> • English · Kiswahili · Français
>
> And it is built for real conditions — mid-range phones, mobile networks, low bandwidth. The same discipline I apply on every Africa-facing build.
>
> As the CHACADOM team puts it: "The KEJA Trust Score has become the number our users check first — 70 listings already certified above 80 in the first months of public trial."
>
> Keja AI is also nominated for an Africa AI Future Award (ceremony 27 Nov 2026, Nairobi).

## Tapi Learn case study — LinkedIn + X

### LinkedIn — long-form (1,895 characters, limit 3,000)

> 11,000 learners don't care what model is under the hood.
> They care that at 2 a.m., in Kiswahili, before an interview, the platform had their back.
>
> That is the design brief behind the AI layer I built for ACLA's Tapi Learn — a free, Africa-built, multilingual learning platform for climate leadership.
>
> What I built:
> → A 24/7 AI Career Coach — CV reviews, interview prep and career advice on demand, in the learner's language, at the moment they need it. It never closes.
> → The EN · FR · SW NLP pipeline — three languages as first-class citizens, including Kiswahili, which most global AI tooling treats as an afterthought.
> → Personalized learning recommendations — "what should I learn next?" gets a real answer, not a syllabus order.
> → An opportunities board — so the platform's last screen is a door, not a dead end.
>
> The design decision that mattered most: most platforms bolt a chatbot onto a course catalog and call it AI. Tapi Learn started from the learner's career problem and worked backwards. That is why the coach feels like the product instead of a support widget.
>
> In ACLA's words: "Learners treat the coach as part of the product, not a support widget."
>
> The results (client-reported, October 2026):
> • 11,000+ learners
> • 38 countries
> • 4.8/5 average rating
> • 24/7 coaching in 3 languages — no queue, no office hours
>
> The business takeaway: if you run a learning, membership or community product, the retention math changes when every user gets guidance, not just content. And if your users are multilingual, treating their language as first-class is not a localization cost — it is market expansion. Three languages took one product into 38 countries.

## FirstLight public launch — LinkedIn + X

### LinkedIn — long-form (1,694 characters, limit 3,000)

> The first six hours after a disaster decide the next six weeks.
>
> Today I am launching FirstLight — a Geo-AI decision layer for disaster response. It turns raw imagery into a prioritized, routable response map for the people who have to make decisions while the picture is still forming.
>
> The pipeline:
> 1. Damage & flood detections fused with road networks, population grids and critical facilities
> 2. Uber H3 need-score zoning — every hexagon gets a priority score with tunable weights: population, damage, hospital access — you set them
> 3. Dijkstra routing on the post-event road graph, hospital isochrones, isolation analysis — which neighborhoods are cut off, and how to reach them
> 4. Grounded situation reports — every number carries its provenance and an FNV-1a hash, so a sitrep is a claim you can inspect, not a score you take on faith
>
> One thing I care about: the AI does not get the last word. Low-confidence detections go to a human-in-the-loop review queue. In disaster response, "the model said so" is not an operational answer.
>
> Honesty section: this public demo is fully client-side. Every artifact is procedurally simulated and labeled with its provenance — no operational data is processed. When you build for disasters, you prove the pipeline on labeled simulations before you ever touch real imagery.
>
> Stack: Next.js · TypeScript · MapLibre GL · Uber H3 · Geo-AI (SAR)
>
> Try it: gadda00.github.io/firstlight
> Read the code: github.com/gadda00/firstlight
>
> If you work in emergency operations, disaster tech or geospatial intelligence — I would like to hear what your first six hours actually look like. That is the problem I built this for.
>
